Abstract

End-to-end pursuit without privileged obstacle information

Decentralized cooperative pursuit in cluttered environments remains difficult under partial and noisy perception. Existing methods often depend on abstract geometric features or privileged ground-truth states, which side-step the real perception uncertainty faced by aerial swarms. We propose a decentralized end-to-end multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that maps raw LiDAR observations directly to continuous control commands. Its core representation, the Predictive Spatio-Temporal Observation, aligns obstacle geometry, predicted evader intent, and teammate motion in a unified egocentric grid. Built on PSTO, a single decentralized policy navigates static obstacles, intercepts dynamic targets, maintains cooperative encirclement, and scales across different team sizes without retraining. Fully autonomous outdoor experiments further validate the framework using only onboard sensing and onboard computing.

Highlights

What makes the framework stand out

01

PSTO representation

PSTO projects raw LiDAR geometry, predicted evader motion, and teammate dynamics onto a shared egocentric grid so the policy can jointly reason about pursuit and collision avoidance.

02

One policy across team sizes

The final parameter-shared decentralized policy transfers from 2-vs-1 to 3-vs-1 and 4-vs-1 pursuit without parameter fine-tuning.

03

Real-world deployment

The system runs on custom quadrotors with onboard LiDAR sensing and onboard computation, demonstrating zero-shot outdoor pursuit in cluttered environments.

Method

A clean pipeline from perception to decentralized control

Each pursuer builds PSTO from local sensing and shared teammate kinematics, while CTDE training with MAPPO improves coordination through a centralized critic and a decentralized actor.

Decentralized execution

Each agent acts from local observations, using raw LiDAR, proprioception, and shared teammate states to produce continuous velocity commands.

Predictive intent modeling

Evader futures and teammate motion are projected into a common grid to provide explicit spatial cues for interception, separation, and encirclement.

CTDE optimization

The actor runs locally at deployment, while the centralized critic uses global information during MAPPO training to improve sample efficiency and coordination.

Results

Strong simulation performance and scalable teamwork

The framework maintains strong success rates in cluttered pursuit, reduces conservative detours compared with disjoint representations, and preserves performance as the number of pursuers increases.

94%+ Success rate across tested 2-vs-1 conditions
89%+ Success rate across team sizes at 2.0 m/s

2-vs-1 to 4-vs-1 One unified policy without parameter changes

Key observations

  • PSTO keeps success rates above 94% across tested 2-vs-1 pursuit conditions without retuning.
  • Spatial alignment between geometry and intent helps agents take efficient shortcuts through clutter instead of passive trailing.
  • The same final policy generalizes across 2-vs-1, 3-vs-1, and 4-vs-1 team sizes.
  • Outdoor experiments confirm zero-shot transfer using only onboard sensing and onboard computing.

Deployment

Validated outdoors on custom quadrotors

  • Custom quadrotor platform with Livox Mid-360 and Intel NUC 13.
  • Average onboard latency of about 3.87 ms for encoding and policy inference.
  • Virtual boundary points are injected into LiDAR observations to match the training arena constraints.
  • Zero-shot transfer produces coordinated pincer-style pursuit and collision-free encirclement in an unstructured outdoor environment.
