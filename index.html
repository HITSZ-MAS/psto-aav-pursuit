Overview

Why this problem matters

Decentralized cooperative pursuit is hard because each aerial agent must act from partial, noisy, egocentric observations while still avoiding obstacles, intercepting a dynamic target, and coordinating with teammates in real time.

Perception uncertainty

Existing pursuit pipelines often assume clean geometric states or privileged obstacle maps, which side-steps the uncertainty of real onboard sensing.

End-to-end control

Our framework maps raw LiDAR directly to continuous actions, avoiding hand-crafted abstractions and enabling perception-to-control learning in cluttered environments.

Swarm scalability

A single decentralized policy scales across different team sizes, allowing 2-vs-1, 3-vs-1, and 4-vs-1 pursuit without retraining.

Method

PSTO unifies geometry, intent, and coordination

The Predictive Spatio-Temporal Observation aligns dense obstacle geometry with predicted evader motion and teammate dynamics in a shared egocentric grid, enabling a policy to reason about pursuit and collision avoidance together.

PSTO representation

One channel encodes obstacle proximity from raw LiDAR, while the other fuses evader attraction and teammate repulsion into a predictive intent heatmap.

Decentralized execution

Each pursuer constructs PSTO from onboard sensing and shared teammate kinematics, then outputs continuous control commands using only local observations.

CTDE training

The actor runs with local observations, while a centralized critic uses global state during MAPPO training to improve coordination and sample efficiency.

Contributions

What the project contributes

Unified observation design

PSTO bridges sparse predictive intent and dense environmental geometry by projecting both onto the same fixed-resolution spherical grid.

Policy reuse across team sizes

The input-invariant structure of PSTO enables one policy to coordinate different team compositions without parameter changes.

Infrastructure-free deployment

The final system is validated on custom quadrotors using only onboard LiDAR sensing and onboard computing.

Results

Strong performance in simulation and scalable coordination

The method maintains high success rates in cluttered pursuit, improves capture efficiency over strong learning-based baselines, and preserves performance as the team grows from 2 pursuers to 4 pursuers.

Key takeaways

  • More than 94% success rate across tested 2-vs-1 pursuit conditions without retuning.
  • PSTO reduces conservative detours by spatially aligning geometry with navigational intent.
  • Compared with disjoint representations, the method improves agility and capture efficiency in clutter.
  • At 2.0 m/s, the same final policy preserves strong performance across 2-vs-1, 3-vs-1, and 4-vs-1 settings.

Real-World Validation

Outdoor deployment with onboard sensing and computing

The policy is deployed on custom-built quadrotors in an unstructured outdoor environment and runs fully onboard with LiDAR sensing, onboard state estimation, and decentralized execution.

  • Custom quadrotor platform with Livox Mid-360 and Intel NUC 13.
  • Average onboard latency of about 3.87 ms for encoding plus inference.
  • Virtual boundary points are added directly into LiDAR observations to match the training arena.
  • Zero-shot transfer demonstrates coordinated pincer-style pursuit and collision-free encirclement outdoors.
